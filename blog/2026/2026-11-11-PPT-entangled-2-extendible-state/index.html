Let \(\lbrace \ket{0},\ket{1},\ket{2}\rbrace\) be the computational basis of a qutrit system, and consider the following quantum state on two qutrits defined in [1]:

\begin{align} \rho &= \frac{2}{7} \ket{\psi}bra{\psi} + \frac{1}{2} (\sigma_+) + \frac{3}{14}(\sigma_-)\\ \text{where}\quad \ket{\psi} &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} (\ket{00}+\ket{11}+\ket{22})\\ \sigma_+ &= \frac{1}{3} (\ket{01}\bra{01}+\ket{12}\bra{12}_\ket{20}\bra{20})\\ \sigma_- &= \frac{1}{3} (\ket{10}\bra{10}+\ket{21}\bra{21}+\ket{02}\bra{02}). \label{eq:state} \end{align}

This state is PPT and entangled by the results of [1].

To construct a (symmetric) 2-extension of \(\rho\), we first consider the following vectors that are symmetric in the 2nd and 3rd system:

\begin{align} \ket{\chi_0} &= \ket{0}\otimes\ket{00} + \ket{1}\otimes(\ket{01}+\ket{10})+\ket{2}\otimes(\ket{02}+ ket{20})\\ \ket{\chi_1} &= \ket{0}\otimes(\ket{01}+\ket{10}) + \ket{1}\otimes\ket{1} + \ket{2}\otimes(\ket{12}+ ket{21})\\ \ket{chi_2} &= \ket{0}\otimes(\ket{02}+\ket{20})+\ket{1}\otimes(\ket{12}+\ket{21})+\ket{2}\otimes\ket{22}. \end{align}

The following state \(\Omega\) satisfies \(\operatorname{tr}_2 \Omega = \operatorname{tr}_3 \Omega = \rho\), and is hence a symmetric 2-extension of \(\rho\):

\begin{align} \Omega &= \frac{2}{63}(\ket{\chi_0}\bra{\chi_0} + \ket{\chi_1}\bra{\chi_1} + \ket{\chi_2}\bra{\chi_2})\\ & \quad {} + \frac{17}{126} (\ket{011}\bra{011} + \ket{122}\bra{122} + \ket{200}\bra{200})\\ & \quad {} + \frac{5}{126} (\ket{100}\bra{100} + \ket{211}\bra{211} + \ket{022}\bra{022}). \end{align}

The marginal of \(\rho\) on the first system is maximally mixed, which shows that \(\rho\) is a bona fide Choi state. Since a channel is antidegradable if and only if its Choi operator is 2-extendible [2], the operator \(\rho\) is the Choi state of a channel that is PPT and antidegradable, but not entanglement-breaking.
